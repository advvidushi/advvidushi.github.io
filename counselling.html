Students and graduates

Counselling

A quiet conversation about whether law is the right path, and, if it is, how to walk it with honesty.

Before college

Those who wish to enter law

School-leavers and graduates from other disciplines sometimes need a clear picture of what an LL.B. demands: reading, ethics, language, and years of formation. Counselling here is informational. It explains pathways, typical stages of study, and the difference between academic interest and a professional life. It does not recruit for a college and does not guarantee admission.

In college

Those already reading law

Law-college students may write for help in planning the years ahead: internships, moots as academic exercises, research habits, teaching and academia, chambers, corporate legal work, public service, and judicial-service study. The conversation is about options and preparation. It is not a job offer and not a placement cell.

Maps, not promises

Subjects a session may cover

Academia

LL.M. and teaching routes, research hygiene, and the temperament required for a life among books and students.

Work and jobs

How to read a vacancy, prepare a curriculum vitae without exaggeration, and understand the difference between internships and employment.

The profession

What enrolment, AIBE, and the first years in court or in an office generally involve — stated as general information, not as an invitation to instruct counsel.

Career counselling is educational. It does not create an advocate-client relationship, does not advise on a personal legal dispute, and does not assure any appointment, stipend, or examination result.
